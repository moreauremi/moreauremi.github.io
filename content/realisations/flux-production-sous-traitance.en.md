## Context

At 1Life, new consultants train on Open-Prod with demo databases before working with customers. On 16 October 2026, I presented a complete flow in Open-Prod to the whole consulting team, the technical director, the project director and the regional director: from the sale of a manufactured product to its invoice, with one step outsourced to a subcontractor.

To make the flow concrete, a simple example: making and selling a leather bag.

## Implementation

### The scenario

A customer orders a bag. To make it, you need leather and buttons, bought from suppliers.

- **Bill of materials**: the list of the bag's components, the leather and the buttons.
- **Routing**: the sequence of manufacturing operations, micro-perforation then assembly, before packaging: wrapping the bag, outsourced to a subcontractor.

### The flow in Open-Prod

1. **The sale**: the customer's order for the bag.
2. **The manufacturing order (MO)**, created as a draft, then confirmed.
3. **The first work order (WO)**: confirming it creates a draft purchase order (PO) for the raw materials, the leather and the buttons.
4. **Buying the raw materials**: confirming the purchase order.
5. **The consumption and production declarations** of the first work order.
6. **The second work order.**
7. **The third work order, subcontracted**: it triggers a subcontracting purchase, created as a draft, then confirmed.
8. **The round trip to the subcontractor**: processing the delivery note, which sends the bag to the subcontractor, then the goods receipt when it comes back.
9. **Finished production**: the bag is complete.
10. **Delivery to the customer**: processing the sale's delivery note.
11. **The invoices**: the raw-material supplier's, the subcontractor's and the customer's. The flow is complete.

Production is declared in two ways: in the form view, and in the **MES view** (for *Manufacturing Execution System*, the shop-floor tracking system), a simplified view for tablets, used with a **barcode scanner**. The work order has its own PDF report, laid out with QWeb, with QR codes. The stock movements generated at each step can be seen in Open-Prod.
